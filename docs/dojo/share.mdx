An app starts private: only the person who shared it can open it. Open the app's **Share** dialog, or use `frogbot dojo sharing`, to let others in.

## Levels

| Level | Can |
| --- | --- |
| Viewer | Opens and uses the app. |
| Editor | Also changes it: deploys, environment variables, and pulling its code. |
| Admin | Also shares it, connects its repo, sets its domain and deletes it. An app always keeps at least one Admin. |

The API and the CLI name these levels `use`, `editor` and `owner`.

## Who can open it

- **Only people invited.** The default. Invite people by email; Dojo emails each one, and they open the app once they sign in with that email. People outside the workspace can be Viewers or Editors, never Admins.
- **Anyone in this workspace.** Every member gets the app at the level their [workspace role](/dojo/overview#workspace-roles) carries: Viewers as Viewers, Editors as Editors, Admins and the Owner as Admins. This needs the Business plan; without it the app is treated as private.
- **Public link.** Anyone with the address opens it as a Viewer, without a Dojo account. What they see is still up to the app's own access rules.

On Business, people on a [project's](/dojo/overview#how-dojo-is-organized) list also get its apps.

## From the terminal

```bash
npx frogbot dojo sharing invite deals ana@example.com --level editor
npx frogbot dojo sharing list deals
npx frogbot dojo sharing leave deals
```

Apps shared with you show under **Shared with you** in Dojo, and in `frogbot dojo apps list`.
